Capital Return vs Profit Distribution

This study models profit only. It does not solve how the original $2,000,000 capital is returned.

Returning capital from a U.S. C corporation needs careful planning. Distributions may be dividends to the extent of earnings and profits. Redemptions, liquidations, and stock sales have separate rules. If the U.S. corporation primarily owns U.S. real estate, stock sale or liquidation mechanics may also raise FIRPTA concerns.

Practical Feasibility Conclusion

Under the updated four-owner split and a 30% total modeled net-profit target, the venture needs about $1,133,137 of pre-tax U.S. corporate profit.

That means the venture should underwrite deals using this rule of thumb:

Underwriting TargetPractical Meaning
Less than 40% pre-tax profit on capitalLikely weak for this structure if the business target is 30% total modeled net profit
Around 56.7% pre-tax profit on capitalApproximate break-even point for the stated 30% total modeled net target
More than 60% pre-tax profit on capitalBetter fit, assuming execution risk is controlled

The key drafting decision is whether the split is intended to be a legal/economic split before owner-side taxes, or a tax-equalized split after owner-side taxes. That should be decided before term sheets, shareholder agreements, or investor decks are finalized.
